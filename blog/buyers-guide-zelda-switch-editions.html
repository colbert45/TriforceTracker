A Buyer's Guide to Existing Zelda Switch Special Editions

Published August 22, 2026 · Ocarina Hardware Watch

If you're waiting on the rumored Ocarina hardware but want something now — or you just want to know what's actually out there before hunting secondhand — here's what to know about the existing Zelda-branded Switch hardware on the market today.

The main option: the Tears of the Kingdom OLED

Covered in detail in our hardware timeline — this is the only full Zelda-branded Switch console Nintendo has released to date. If you're buying new, check current retail availability directly, since limited-run hardware can come and go from a given retailer's stock without warning. If you're buying secondhand, the checks below matter more than usual.

What to check before buying secondhand

  • Included accessories — confirm whether the listing includes the matching dock, Joy-Con or Pro Controller, and any included case. Special-edition bundles get split up and resold piecemeal more often than people expect.
  • Screen condition — OLED screens can develop burn-in with heavy use of static UI elements over a long period. Ask for a photo with a plain gray or white background displayed if you can't inspect it in person.
  • Joy-Con drift — a well-documented issue across Switch hardware generally, not specific to special editions. Worth asking directly rather than assuming a listing's "great condition" claim covers it.
  • Authenticity of the box art and cosmetic finish — special editions attract counterfeit shells and stickers more than standard hardware does, precisely because the cosmetic details are the entire value proposition. Compare closely against official product photos before paying a premium over a standard console.

On pricing

We're deliberately not quoting specific resale numbers here — secondhand pricing for limited-run hardware fluctuates with supply, demand, and platform generation in ways that make any figure we'd write today stale within weeks. Cross-reference a few different marketplaces rather than anchoring to the first listing you see.

Should you wait for the rumored hardware instead?

This is our opinion, not advice: if what you actually want is a piece of history tied to Ocarina of Time specifically, and the leaked hardware turns out to be exactly that, waiting probably makes sense. If you just want a nice-looking Zelda Switch to use now, the existing TotK OLED is a real, available option today rather than a rumor. Depends what you're actually optimizing for.
