A Timeline of Every Zelda-Themed Nintendo Hardware Release

Published August 20, 2026 · Ocarina Hardware Watch

Before we get any deeper into the rumored Switch 2 hardware, it's worth stepping back and looking at what Nintendo has actually shipped with Zelda branding over the years. This list only counts hardware that got an official regional release — not fan mods, not preorder bonus discs, not the occasional trade-show display unit that never made it to shelves.

YearHardwareNotes
2003Game Boy Advance SP, Zelda Limited EditionGold shell with a Triforce on the lid, released around the Four Swords era. Availability varied a lot by region.
2011Nintendo 3DS, Zelda 25th Anniversary EditionGold with a Hyrule Crest design, timed to Ocarina of Time 3D. As best we can tell this one was Europe/Australia-only and never got a proper North American release.
2011Gold Wii Remote Plus (Skyward Sword bundle)Not a full console, but the accessory bundle that shipped alongside Skyward Sword's launch.
2015New Nintendo 3DS XL, Majora's Mask EditionLid art built around Majora's Mask, released to match Majora's Mask 3D.
2023Nintendo Switch OLED, Tears of the Kingdom EditionBlack-and-gold dock and console with a glow-in-the-dark Triforce, plus a matching Pro Controller. The most elaborate Zelda special edition to date.

What's notably missing

If you scanned that table expecting to see Breath of the Wild, you're not misremembering — there wasn't a dedicated special-edition Switch for it, despite BotW being one of the best-selling and most acclaimed games in the series. There were amiibo and some accessory bundles, but no console got the full paint job. This next part is speculation, not something we can confirm: our best guess is that the Switch itself was such a runaway success on its own in 2017 that Nintendo didn't feel it needed a collector's-edition console to move units. That's a guess, not a documented reason.

The pattern that does hold up across this list is that special editions tend to cluster around anniversaries (the 25th in 2011) or major single-game launches (Skyward Sword, Majora's Mask 3D, Tears of the Kingdom) rather than showing up on any predictable yearly schedule.
