A History of Zelda Special Edition Hardware

Published August 19, 2026 · Ocarina Hardware Watch

Nintendo has been putting Zelda's gold-and-green color scheme on hardware since the Game Boy Advance days, and by our count there are close to 20 special-edition Switch models alone tied to specific game launches, several of them Zelda-themed. That history is actually useful — Nintendo tends to follow a fairly consistent playbook, and the most recent example gives us a detailed timeline to work from.

Case study: the Tears of the Kingdom OLED

This is the most relevant precedent we have, since it's the most recent Zelda hardware special edition and closely documented.

DateEvent
Late Dec 2022First leaked photos surface on a fan forum, later spreading to Reddit and ResetEra
Mar 28, 2023Officially announced during a Tears of the Kingdom gameplay showcase — about 3 months after the leak
Apr 28, 2023Special-edition console launches — 2 weeks before the game itself
May 12, 2023Matching Pro Controller, carrying case, and the game all launch together

A few patterns jump out. First, the gap between leak and official confirmation was almost exactly three months — not fast, but not glacial either. Second, and this is the part we think matters most for what's coming: the console itself launched ahead of the game, not alongside it, so early adopters could unbox and set it up before the software dropped. Third, the accessories (controller, case) were held back to launch day itself, separate from the console's own launch.

It's not just a Tears of the Kingdom thing

The same "hardware slightly ahead of the game" pattern shows up elsewhere in Nintendo's back catalog too — the Animal Crossing: New Horizons Switch released about a week early, and the Splatoon 3 OLED released two weeks early, matching the Zelda timing almost exactly. This looks less like a Zelda-specific choice and more like a standard Nintendo release-logistics habit.

So what does this suggest for the Ocarina hardware?

This section is speculation, not a prediction we're confident in — treat it as informed guesswork, not fact. If the current leak follows the same rhythm as 2022–23:

  • An official announcement, if it comes, would plausibly land inside a Nintendo Direct or similar showcase rather than a standalone press release — that's been Nintendo's preferred venue for hardware reveals tied to a specific game
  • If the hardware is genuinely Ocarina-tied, expect it to ship ahead of the remake itself by one to a few weeks, not simultaneously
  • A matching Pro Controller launching on the same day as the game, separate from the console's own release, would match precedent

The bigger unresolved question, which we covered in our hardware roundup, is whether this is Ocarina-specific at all versus a broader 40th-anniversary product that isn't tied to any single game's release schedule. If it's the latter, all of the above timing logic doesn't really apply — anniversary hardware has historically launched on its own calendar, not a game's.

We'll update this post if better historical comparisons surface, and keep the live status tracker current as real news breaks.
