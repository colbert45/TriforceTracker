Ocarina of Time at 30: Why It Still Gets Remembered as a Landmark

Published August 21, 2026 · Ocarina Hardware Watch

Ocarina of Time released on the Nintendo 64 in November 1998, which means its actual 30th anniversary lands in 2028 — a couple of years out from where we are now. But with a remake rumored for hardware we've been tracking closely, it felt like the right moment to ask a question that's easy to take for granted: why does this specific game still get talked about as a landmark, rather than just a fondly-remembered entry in a long-running series?

It wasn't just "Zelda but 3D"

Plenty of 2D franchises made the jump to 3D around the same era and the results were mixed. Ocarina of Time is remembered less for making that jump and more for how it solved problems that hadn't really been solved yet — targeting and lock-on combat being the most cited example, a system that a huge number of later action games, inside and outside Nintendo, borrowed from directly or indirectly.

The time-travel structure was genuinely novel for the series

Splitting the game into a child and adult version of the same Hyrule wasn't just a gimmick — it let the game reuse its world while making it feel meaningfully different, and it's the mechanical root of the entire timeline-branching conversation we walked through in our timeline explainer. Later games have used time or dimension-shifting mechanics since, but this was an early and influential version of the idea.

Critical consensus at the time was close to unanimous

Ocarina of Time was widely reviewed as close to a perfect score across most major outlets of the era — the kind of near-universal acclaim that's genuinely rare and helped cement its reputation early, rather than it being a game that got re-evaluated as a classic years later.

Why a remake carries so much weight

This section is our own read, not confirmed fact: a lot of the anticipation around the rumored hardware isn't really about hardware at all — it's that Ocarina of Time is one of the few games in the franchise where a full ground-up remake feels like it could genuinely introduce it to a new generation the way the original did in 1998, rather than just being a visual refresh of something people already know well. That's a high bar, and it's one reason the rumors around this specific project have generated more discussion than a typical special-edition console leak would.
