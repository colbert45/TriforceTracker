The Rumored Ocarina of Time Switch 2 Hardware

Published August 19, 2026 · Updated August 22, 2026 · Ocarina Hardware Watch

If you've landed here from a search, here's the short version: the remake itself is real and officially confirmed. What's still just a leak is the matching special-edition hardware — a black/dark-green Switch 2 console with gold Triforce detailing, and a matching Pro Controller. Below is the timeline as we understand it, and we'll keep updating it as new information surfaces.

What's confirmed

Nintendo officially revealed The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time remake for Switch 2 during a June 9, 2026 Nintendo Direct, confirming a 2026 release with no specific date — straight from Nintendo's own news page. That's the one fully confirmed, non-speculative fact in this whole story. Everything from here down is leak, rumor, or our own reading of the pattern.

DateEvent
Jun 9, 2026Remake officially announced during a Nintendo Direct, targeting a 2026 window with no specific date. Source: Nintendo's own news page.
Jun 14, 2026Nintendo Everything reports Nintendo's marketing language for the remake: "stunning visuals, updated designs, and timeless gameplay."
Jul 29, 2026ESRB rating revealed: E10+ for "Animated Blood and Fantasy Violence." Notably absent: "Suggestive Themes," which the 2011 3DS remake carried, tied to the Great Fairy character.
Late Jul 2026Leak framing shifts from "Ocarina-specific" special edition toward broader "Zelda 40th anniversary" branding, per leaker Shpeshal Nick, corroborated by multiple outlets.
Aug 11, 2026Box art for the black/dark-green/gold-Triforce Switch 2 console and matching Pro Controller circulates online, corroborated by Nintendo Life, Kotaku, TechRadar, 9to5Toys, and NintendoReporters, plus journalist Necro Felipe, who says he's seen the products firsthand.

What's still just leaked

As of this writing, Nintendo has not officially confirmed the special-edition hardware, its price, or any release date for it. The box art is corroborated by several outlets with track records on past Nintendo leaks, plus a journalist claiming firsthand contact with the products — stronger sourcing than a single blurry photo, but still not the same thing as Nintendo confirming it directly. Leaked box art alone has been a reasonably reliable predictor in the past — the Tears of the Kingdom OLED leaked in similar fashion months before Nintendo confirmed it — but Nintendo has also let leaked designs die quietly before.

The detail everyone's skipping past

Here's the thing worth being upfront about: several of the sources covering this leak aren't actually calling it Ocarina-specific anymore. As of late July 2026, the framing shifted toward general "Zelda 40th anniversary" branding, which could just as easily ship as its own standalone collector's item — the way Nintendo has done 35th and 40th anniversary Mario hardware — without being tied to the Ocarina remake's release at all. It's entirely possible we get a 40th-anniversary console months before or after the remake itself, the way the TOTK OLED beat its own game to shelves by two weeks. Worth tempering expectations that these are guaranteed to launch together.

A small, speculative detail worth flagging

This part is speculation, not anything confirmed: the remake's ESRB rating notably drops the "Suggestive Themes" descriptor that the 2011 3DS remake carried, and several outlets have connected that to the Great Fairy character possibly being redesigned. It's a reasonable guess based on the rating language alone, but it's still just a guess — ESRB descriptors can shift for reasons that have nothing to do with any one character.

Why the Pro Controller matters more than it seems

Nintendo doesn't always pair a special-edition console with a matching accessory line — sometimes it's console-only. The fact that leaks are showing a controller too suggests Nintendo is treating this as a bigger merchandising moment than a quiet reskin, which historically has correlated with earlier, bigger marketing pushes (Direct segments, dedicated trailers) rather than a footnote in a larger showcase.

What we're watching for next

  • Any further Nintendo Direct or showcase scheduling that would give a natural window for a hardware-specific reveal
  • Retailer listing leaks — the next stage up from a box art leak, and a historically reliable tell in its own right
  • Whether Nintendo's own messaging starts using "Ocarina Edition" specifically versus generic "40th Anniversary" language
